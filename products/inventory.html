Loom & fabric stock

Every roll, from the loom to the lorry

A woven fabric plant loses rolls in the gap between the loom and the godown — read on a slip, cut without a number, weighed on a scale nobody wrote down, stacked somewhere and found two months later. Nexora Fabric Inventory closes that gap: the loom reading, the cut, the weight off the scale, the label on the roll, the stock, the packing slip and the dispatch, each one knowing the one before it — on the PC and on the phone.

The idea

A roll is not a row in a table. It is a story.

Nothing here keeps a "quantity on hand" field. Every figure you see — a quality's stock, a day's opening, an operator's output — is worked out from the things that actually happened to each roll. That is why a day you close today still reads the same when you open it next month.

The loom is read on a slotA date and a time, one row per running loom. The previous reading and the operator arrive by themselves; production works itself out as you type.
The roll is cut or receivedCut off a loom, which gives it its quality, its operator and its metres — or received from outside, quality by quality, on the same roll series.
It is weighed where it is madeThe scale's own figure, at the machine. A roll with a weight goes into stock; one without stays pending until somebody weighs it.
It stands in stock with its whole historyScan or type the roll number and you get the loom, the shift, the operator, the weighing, the packing and the dispatch, on one screen.
The dashboard

Short by default, open what you want

Today or any month of the last two years. Six figures across the top, then every other part is one line with its number on it — click the line and it opens. What you left open, and the period you left it on, is how you find it next morning.

The reading that cannot lie

Loom readings are where fabric accounting usually breaks: a counter typed wrong, a roll cut nobody recorded, a loom that stood all evening with no one saying why. Each of those is refused or questioned at the moment it is typed.

  • A reading below the previous one, with no roll cut between them, is never saved — the row turns red and the slot stays open
  • A loom that stood is not saved without why it stood; the reasons are your plant's own, kept under Settings → Production
  • Only an administrator may set a row's previous and current readings, and the row keeps the reason they gave for it
  • Roll cuts made during the slot are shown against the loom, so the metres always add up
  • Your own reading order. A hundred looms are not read 1, 2, 3 — type "1-6, 11-16, 7-10" once and the computer, the phone and the "not read" warning all walk the floor the way your reader does

Efficiency, from the loom's own speed

A loom's speed is in picks a minute (shuttles × RPM) and a quality has its picks per inch. From those two the application knows what that loom should make in an hour, and compares it with what it did make in the minutes it actually ran.

expected m/hour = picks per minute × 60 ÷ (picks per inch × 39.37)
efficiency    = production ÷ expected, for the minutes the loom ran

Efficiency shows per row and per slot as you type. A reading keeps the speed and the picks per inch as they were on the day, so re-tuning a loom next year does not quietly rewrite last year's efficiency.

Weighing

The scale is at the machine, not in a separate window

There is no "roll weighing" screen to go and find. The weight is taken where the roll is made — in Roll Cutting and in Roll Receipt — because that is the one moment somebody is standing next to it.

What the scale gives, and what you type

  • A stable weight comes into the gross by itself — and only after the scale has been emptied since the last roll, so one roll's weight can never land on the next
  • Take takes the reading again; a person given the right may type a weight by hand when the scale is down
  • The tare is typed every time, because a core is not always the same core
  • Net, GPM, gram and GSM are worked out from the quality's size and whether it is tubular or flat
  • GPM is held against that quality's ± tolerance; outside it the figure turns red and saving asks you first

Any scale, and one for training

A scale on RS-232 or USB-serial, read by the window itself — nothing extra to install on the computer. Port, speed, data bits, parity and stop bits are yours to set, and so is how your scale writes a weight: continuously, the A&D ST,GS,… form, a plain number, or a pattern you give it.

Several set-ups can be kept with one in use, each computer with its own. There is a simulator too, so a new operator can be trained and the whole chain tried on a machine that has no scale attached at all.

Settings → Weight scale, with a live test against the last line your scale actually sent.

The label that makes the roll findable

A roll with a printed label is a roll anybody can identify at the far end of the godown with a phone. Printed straight to a thermal label printer when the weight is saved — no dialog, no waiting.

  • Five designs to choose from — Classic, QR left, Big figures, Boxed, Dark band — each drawn for the size you print at
  • A QR carrying the roll number first, then quality, weights, metres, GPM, GSM and gram — readable by any phone camera, no app needed — and a Code 128 barcode if you want one too
  • Your logo on it, or not
  • Label width and height, the gap between labels, and a nudge right or down — per computer, because the printer on the shop floor is not the one in the office

Fabric Stock, and what is behind every line

In stock, pending weight, or every roll ever made. Search it or scan a roll, filter by quality and inward date, and see each quality's totals.

The stock table carries

Item code • quality • size • gram • GPM • GSM • date of inward • metres • issued kg • current stock

A GPM outside its tolerance stays red in the list, so a bad roll is visible without opening it. Click any roll and you get its details and its traceability together — the loom it came off and the operators whose metres made it, when it was cut or received, how it was weighed and by whom, when it went into stock, and which packing slip and dispatch took it out. Excel of the lot, whenever you want it.

The day, closed properly

A stock sheet that still agrees with itself next year

Quality by quality, the oldest arithmetic in the trade — and every figure in it worked out from the rolls themselves rather than from a running total somebody could have nudged.

opening + in (weighed) − dispatched − issued + returned = closing

Kg, metres or rolls

One at a time on the screen so a column means one thing; the Excel has all three side by side.

A half roll is still a roll

Issue part of a roll and the balance adds a roll to the count, not a fraction of one — because that is what is standing in the godown.

It ties back

The closing figure equals Fabric Stock's current stock. If the two ever disagreed, one of them would be a running total — and neither is.

Out of the gate, on paper

The packing slip is made from rolls you pick — scanned, typed, or the oldest of a quality taken by FIFO — and it carries its own dispatch with the vehicle, driver, transporter, LR, invoice and e-way bill. The dispatch register is the list of what left and when. Returns and internal issues come back through the same ledger, so the stock never has to be corrected by hand.

  • A roll that was partly issued inside the plant cannot go out on a packing slip at its full weight — it is refused, and told how many kilograms have already gone
  • A dispatch counts at what was actually left on the roll
  • The slip previews on screen and prints as a slip, not as a web page

The shift report, and who ran it

A date and a shift, loom by loom: quality, operator, production, minutes stood and why, efficiency, rolls cut, totals and signature lines. Printed on A4 or saved as a PDF — the sheet a supervisor hands over at the end of a shift.

Operator performance sits beside it: each person's readings, looms, hours run and minutes stood, production with a bar, metres an hour, efficiency, how far they are from the plant's own average, and what they stood for most.

It is there to look at, not to pay by. That is deliberate. A number that decides somebody's wages gets gamed, and then it stops telling you anything about the looms.

Reports

Any report, from a date to a date

Production by day or by shift, loom performance, operator performance, why the looms stood, quality-wise production, rolls cut and received, dispatch by customer. Each with its figures, a table and a total line — and out to Excel, Print or PDF.

Quick, or exact

Today, yesterday, 7 or 30 days, this month, last month, or any month from the list — and From / To for anything else.

The same dates everywhere

Production Records, the Stock Sheet, Operator Performance and the Dispatch Register all gained the same quick dates, so you are never re-learning a filter.

Excel, Print, PDF

Every report leaves the application in the form the person asking for it actually wanted.

Frequently asked

Is Fabric Inventory part of Nexora Sales & Costing, or its own application?

Its own. It installs separately, keeps its own data and runs on its own, so a weaving unit that does not cost bags can use it alone. It wears the same look, the same menus and the same rules about who may do what, so anybody who already uses Nexora is not learning a second application.

What kind of weighing scale does it work with?

A scale on RS-232 or USB-serial. The window reads it directly, so there is nothing extra to install on the computer. You set the port, speed, data bits, parity and stop bits, and how your scale writes a weight — continuously, the A&D ST,GS,… form, a plain number, or a pattern you give it. Several set-ups can be saved with one in use, and each computer keeps its own.

What if we do not have a scale yet, or it breaks?

There is a simulator, so the whole chain can be tried and an operator trained on a machine with no scale attached. And a person who is given that right can type a weight by hand when the scale is down — it is recorded as typed, not as weighed, so you can always tell the two apart later.

Does the roll counter reset when a roll is cut?

Whichever your plant does. Some keep the counter running across cuts and some zero it, so it is a setting — for the company, and for a single loom that behaves differently. The metres come out right either way: cut minus previous cut when it keeps running, the cut reading itself when it resets.

Can somebody fix a wrong loom reading?

Only an administrator, and the row keeps the reason they gave. An ordinary reading that falls below the previous one with no roll cut between them is never saved at all — the row turns red and the slot stays open until it is right. That is the one place where being strict saves a month of argument later.

What does a roll label carry?

A QR with the roll number first and then quality, weights, metres, GPM, GSM and gram — any phone camera reads it, no app needed — and a Code 128 barcode as well if you want one. Five designs to pick from, your logo on or off, and the label size, the gap between labels and a nudge set per computer. It prints straight to the thermal printer when the weight is saved, with no dialog in the way.

Can we trace a roll back to the loom and the operator?

Yes, and it is one click. Type or scan the roll number and you get its details and its whole story on one screen: the loom it came off and the operators whose metres made it, when it was cut or received, how it was weighed and by whom, when it went into stock, and which packing slip and dispatch took it out.

Why does the daily stock sheet agree with the stock screen?

Because neither of them is a running total. Both are worked out from what happened to each roll, so the sheet's closing figure and the stock screen's current stock are the same number arrived at twice. It also means a day you closed last month still reads the same today.

Can a roll be dispatched after part of it was issued inside the plant?

Not at its full weight — it is refused, and told how many kilograms have already gone. A dispatch counts at what was actually left on the roll. This was a real fault that got through once; it is now one of the things the service checks before it will accept the document.

Is operator performance meant for calculating wages?

No, and deliberately so. It is there to look at — whose looms run, who stands the longest and what they stand for. A figure that decides somebody's pay gets managed rather than reported, and then it stops telling you anything useful about the looms.

Is there a phone app, and does it do the same things?

Yes — an Android app (Android 8 and later) on the same company, the same people and the same records. Loom readings swiped one loom to a card, roll cutting, stock with a label scan, packing and dispatch, the dashboard and the reports. A phone joins with the company id and passcode and waits for the administrator's approval; its own key never leaves it.

What happens on the phone when there is no signal?

It keeps working. What you save goes into the phone's store and its queue together and is sent at the next sync. If somebody changed the same record first, both copies are kept — yours, with the company's under Versions — and a roll number already taken offline by another phone or computer is given the next free one.

Does Nexora AI send our production figures to Google?

No. Nexora AI writes the search and your own computer runs it on your own records — every answer says so on the screen. What goes out is the question with customers, operators, people and qualities turned into codes like [C1] and [Q1], today's date, the window you are on and what you are allowed to search. A reading, a roll, a weight, a stock or a dispatch figure is never sent.

What can Nexora AI actually do here?

Answer a question about your records in English, ગુજરાતી or हिन्दी with a sentence, the figures and a table; change that table on a follow-up ("only Q-90", "loom wise", "top 5"); open Fabric Stock or Production Records holding exactly the records it found; fill a window from what you said — a slot's readings, receipt rows, a FIFO packing slip — which it never saves, you check and press Save; and work out a new column from a formula you asked for in words.

Does it work without the internet?

Yes. It is an offline-first Windows application; the plant keeps working when the line is down and catches up with the company when it is back. People, rights and the masters are shared company-wide.
