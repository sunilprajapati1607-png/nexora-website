What breaks in a woven sack plant, and what it costs you in output

A woven sack line is a chain, and the chain stops at its weakest machine. Tape extrusion feeds the looms, the looms feed lamination, lamination feeds printing, and printing feeds cutting and stitching. An hour lost at the extruder is not an hour lost at the extruder — it is an hour that the looms behind it eventually stand idle for.

That is why maintenance in this industry is not a spares register. It is a production question, and it has to be answered in the same report as output.

Breakdown logging that a shift can actually do

Maintenance systems fail for one reason: the person on the floor will not fill them in. Nexora keeps the breakdown entry to what a supervisor can finish between two machines — machine, time down, time up, what happened, what was done, what was consumed. The reason is picked from your own list rather than typed, so that six months later the same fault is the same word and can be counted.

Preventive schedules that come to you

A preventive schedule is only preventive if somebody is told. Schedules run on running hours or on the calendar, whichever fits the machine — a loom on hours, a compressor on months — and the due list arrives on the dashboard rather than sitting in a register nobody opens. Overdue work stays visible until it is closed, with who closed it and when.

Spares, and the ones that always run out

Every breakdown records what it consumed, so the spares list stops being a guess. Over a few months the plant learns which parts it truly runs on — shuttles, reeds, needles, doctor blades, filters, belts — and which ones sat on the shelf for a year. Consumption ties back to the machine, so a single loom eating shuttles is visible as a machine problem rather than a stores problem.

Downtime in the same report as output

This is the part that changes decisions. Downtime by machine, by reason and by shift, set beside what that machine produced, answers the questions a plant actually argues about: which machine is really the bottleneck, whether the night shift loses more time than the day shift, whether a fault is getting worse, and whether the last repair held. A machine that is down for a short time every day costs more than one that is down for a day once — and only a report that counts both shows it.

It connects to the rest of the plant

Because maintenance sits in the same system as shop-floor tracking and inventory, a downtime record knows which operator was on the machine and which spare left the store. And because the ERP holds the production plan, a machine that is out of action is a machine the plan can be told about.
