How woven bag weight is calculated

A woven sack is a tube of PP or HDPE tape fabric, cut to length, folded or pasted at the ends, and optionally coated, laminated with BOPP film, metallised and printed. The weight of the bag is the sum of every layer's area multiplied by its GSM. Constructions are named by how many layers carry weight: 1 Layer is bare fabric, 2 Layer adds coating, 3 Layer adds BOPP film, 4 Layer adds a metallised film.

Fabric GSM = Denier × (mesh + mesh) ÷ 228.6  (warp and weft carry the same count)
Film GSM = micron × factor (BOPP 0.92, metallised 0.92)
Tube width = 2 × lay-flat bag width  ·  Cut length = length + bottom fold + hamming (stitched), or length + patch + overlap (block bottom)
Body weight (g) = width × cut length × 2 ÷ 1,000,000 × layer GSM (coating and film spread over width + 10 mm)
Bag weight = body layers + top patch + bottom patch + valve + yarn  (a liner is costed separately)

Bag costing and packaging costing

Cost per bag = raw material kilograms (including process waste) × rate for each material, plus conversion cost per kilogram for extrusion, weaving, lamination, printing and finishing. The rates on this page are examples for demonstration. In production costing, resin and film prices change every week, so a rate must be dated and versioned — an approved quote keeps the rate it was costed at. That is exactly what the RM master in Nexora does, and why the component-wise breakdown here is reserved for the demo.

How to Calculate Woven Bag Cost per Bag vs Cost per Kg?

The market quotes a woven sack in rupees per kilogram. The margin, though, lives in rupees per bag — because the customer buys bags, not kilograms. Both numbers come from the same place, and the woven sack costing formula below is the one every bag costing software has to get right.

kg per bag    = bag weight (g) ÷ 1000 × (1 + process waste %)
material cost = Σ (each layer's kilograms × that material's effective rate)
conversion   = kg per bag × conversion rate per kg (power, manpower, machine)
cost per bag  = material cost + conversion cost
cost per kg   = cost per bag ÷ (bag weight ÷ 1000)

Why cost per kg hides the money

Two quotations can carry the identical rate per kilogram and still differ by a fortune, because the rate says nothing about how heavy the bag is. The specification decides the weight, and the weight decides the resin. Five grams on a 50 × 90 cm sack looks like nothing until it is multiplied by an order.

What changes for BOPP bag costing

A laminated bag adds layers, and every layer carries its own rate. BOPP bag costing has to hold the woven fabric, the extrusion coating that bonds the film, the BOPP film itself, the ink and, on a metallised bag, the adhesive — each priced separately, each with its own waste at its own process. Costing such a bag at one blended rate per kilogram is how plants quietly lose money on their most expensive product.

The rates on this page are examples so you can see the build-up. In production the rate has to be dated: resin and film move every week, and an approved quotation must keep the rate it was costed at. That is the difference between a calculator and a bag costing system.

Frequently asked questions

Costing

What is included in PP woven bag costing?

Raw material and conversion, both. The raw material is the PP or HDPE granule with its masterbatch and filler, the coating resin, the BOPP or metallised film, the printing ink, the stitching thread and any liner. The conversion is what each process costs to run — extrusion, weaving, lamination, printing, cutting and stitching — charged per kilogram that passes through it, with process waste carried forward at every stage because scrap is still paid for.

How is cost per bag calculated?

Work out the fabric area from the cut size, turn that area into grams using the GSM of each layer, add the component weights for patches, valve, ink and thread, then add process waste. That gives the kilograms one bag consumes. Multiply each material's kilograms by its effective rate, add the conversion cost per kilogram, and the total is the cost per bag.

What is the difference between cost per bag and cost per kg?

Cost per kilogram is what the market quotes; cost per bag is what decides the margin, because the customer buys bags. The two are linked by the bag's weight, so the same rate per kilogram produces a different price per bag the moment the specification changes. A five gram difference on 1,00,000 bags is over 500 kg of resin.

How much does process waste add to the cost of a bag?

Directly and in full, because scrap is bought and paid for. Three per cent waste on a 77.6 gram bag adds 2.33 grams of raw material to every bag, which is 233 kilograms on an order of 1,00,000 bags. At ₹105 per kilogram that is roughly ₹24,000 on one order, which is why waste belongs in the costing rather than in a contingency.

How is the conversion cost of a woven sack worked out?

Per process, per kilogram. Each process — tape extrusion, weaving, lamination, printing, cutting, stitching, packing — carries a rate covering its manpower, power and machine time. The kilograms passing through each process are multiplied by that process's rate, so a bag that skips lamination is simply not charged for it.

What waste percentage should I use in a bag costing?

Use your own measured figure rather than a rule of thumb, because it varies by process and by construction. Plants commonly see two to five per cent overall on a plain stitched sack and more on a laminated or printed bag, where lamination and printing add their own losses. The calculator applies one overall figure; the desktop app carries waste per material and per process stage.

How do I cost a BOPP laminated bag?

Cost every layer separately. A BOPP bag carries the woven fabric, the extrusion coating that bonds the film, the BOPP film itself, the ink and, on a metallised bag, the adhesive, and each has its own rate and its own waste. Blending them into one rate per kilogram is how plants lose money on their most expensive product. Use the BOPP bag calculation page for the weight, then apply the rates layer by layer.

How does GSM affect the cost of a bag?

GSM is the cost, in effect. Weight is area multiplied by GSM, and cost is weight multiplied by rate, so every extra gram per square metre flows straight into the price. Dropping fabric GSM from 80 to 78 on a 0.9 square metre bag saves about 1.8 grams per bag, or 180 kilograms of resin on 1,00,000 bags.

How often should raw material rates be updated in a bag costing?

As often as they move, which for resin and film is weekly. What matters more is that each rate is dated: an approved quotation has to keep the rate it was costed at, so a later price change cannot silently rewrite a costing that has already gone to a customer. That is the difference between a calculator and a bag costing system.

No. They are example rates so you can see how material and conversion build up. Enter your own rates in the "Edit example RM prices" panel, or request a demo to cost with your own dated price master and process rates.

Weight and specification

How do I calculate the GSM of woven fabric from denier?

GSM = denier × (mesh + mesh) ÷ 228.6, because warp and weft carry the same count. For 900 denier tape at 10 × 10 mesh: 900 × 20 ÷ 228.6 = 78.7 GSM. Use the denier to GSM calculator for any mesh.

How much does BOPP lamination add to bag weight?

BOPP film GSM is micron × 0.92, so an 18 micron film adds about 16.6 GSM, plus the extrusion coating layer of typically 15 to 22 GSM. See the BOPP bag calculation page.

What is a block bottom bag and how is its weight different?

A block bottom, pasted or pinch bottom bag has a rectangular bottom and top formed from the tube, with pasted patches and optionally a valve. Its cut length includes the patch and the patch overlap, and the patches themselves add weight. See block bottom bag calculation.

What do 1 Layer, 2 Layer, 3 Layer and 4 Layer mean?

They count the layers that carry weight. 1 Layer is bare woven fabric; 2 Layer adds the extrusion coating; 3 Layer adds the BOPP film; 4 Layer adds a metallised film. The calculator only asks for the layers a construction actually has.

What is GPM and how is it different from GSM?

GSM is grams per square metre of fabric; GPM is grams per running metre, which is what the loom operator weighs. GPM equals GSM multiplied by the fabric width in metres, and a tubular fabric counts its lay-flat width twice. Use the GSM to GPM calculator to move between them.

How many bags can I make from one tonne of raw material?

Divide a tonne by the bag weight including waste. A 77.6 gram bag at three per cent waste consumes 79.9 grams, so one tonne yields about 12,500 bags. The calculator shows the reverse figure directly: the kilograms an order of a given quantity will need.

Can I get the weight of each component: body, patch, valve?

The component-wise breakdown, with the formula behind every intermediate value, is a feature of the Nexora desktop app. Request a demo and we will run your own bag specification through it.

Where is the liner in this calculator?

A liner is a separate article with its own lay-flat width, length and seal, so it has its own page: the LDPE liner weight calculator. In the desktop app the liner is one line of the bag's Bill of Materials.

Can I enter sizes in inches or millimetres?

Yes. Every size on every calculator takes millimetres, centimetres or inches, and switching converts the value so the answer does not move. Roll lengths take metres, yards or feet.

Is this calculator accurate enough to quote a customer from?

It is accurate enough to check a quotation and to catch a mistake, using common plant defaults for fold, patch, valve and waste. It is not your plant. Fold allowances, patch depths, valve sizes, ink coverage and waste norms differ from one factory to the next, and a real quotation should run on your own constants and your own dated rates — which is what the desktop app does across 29 constructions.
