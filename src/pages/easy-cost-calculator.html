What the gram is, and why it is not GSM

A bag maker quoting on the phone says "three and a half gram", and everyone in the room knows what is meant. That figure is not grams per square metre. It is the shop-floor number the whole trade sum is built on: width in inches, multiplied by the gram, multiplied by the cut length in inches, divided by 39.37. This calculator keeps the gram exactly as the trade uses it and never converts it, because the point of a quick estimate is that it answers the question you actually asked.

PP woven
cut length = length + 1″ stitching
fabric (g) = width × gram × cut ÷ 39.37 + 1 g thread
lamination (g) = width × 0.5 × cut ÷ 39.37  (if laminated)
cost = fabric×rate÷1000 + lamination×rate÷1000 + printing + fixed + jumbo

BOPP laminated, both sides
plain (g) = width × gram ÷ 39.37 × cut + 1 g thread
coating (g) = width × 0.70 ÷ 39.37 × cut
film (g) = width×25.4 × cut×25.4 × 0.91 × micron × 2 sides ÷ 1,000,000 + 0.2 g trim
price = fabric + coating + film + liner + stitching + making + profit

The fabric rate is not one number: it moves with the gram, with a 19″ roll, with a maxi roll at 36″ and over, and on a BOPP bag with a side gusset. The calculator shows the arithmetic under the result, so you can see which adjustment did what.

Why this will not match a real costing

It cannot, and it should not. A quick sum on the gram treats the bag as fabric plus a film plus a couple of per-bag charges. A real cost per bag is built the other way round: from the construction, every layer's own weight, the route the bag takes through the plant, the recipe at each process, the waste at each stage and the price you actually paid for each raw material on the date you bought it. The two are different arithmetic answering different questions.

FAQ

Are these your rates?

No. Every rate on the page is a round, illustrative sample so that the calculator shows something rather than zero. They are not a quotation, not a market rate and not anybody's price list. Open the sample rates and type your own over them.

Is the gram the same as GSM?

No. The trade gram (2.5 to 4.5) is the figure the shop-floor formula uses; grams per square metre is a different number entirely. This page stays on the gram and never converts it. If you want to work in GSM, micron and denier, use the full bag weight calculator.

Why is one inch added to the length?

For the stitching allowance. The cut length is always a little more than the finished length, and one inch is what the trade sum assumes. Your own allowance may differ.

Why is the thread in the weight but not in the fabric charge?

Because a gram of stitching yarn is not a gram of woven PP and is not bought at the fabric rate. The BOPP sum carries it in the weight and leaves it out of the fabric charge, which is what the trade sheet does.

Is anything I type here saved or sent?

No. The calculator runs entirely in your browser. Nothing is stored and nothing is sent anywhere.
