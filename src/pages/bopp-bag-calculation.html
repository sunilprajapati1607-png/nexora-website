How a BOPP laminated bag is built up

A BOPP bag is a woven PP tube (typically 700–1000 denier at 10 × 10 to 12 × 12 mesh) laminated with a reverse-printed BOPP film through an extrusion coating sandwich. Three layers contribute weight: the woven fabric, the extruded coating that bonds the film, and the BOPP film itself.

Fabric GSM = denier × (warp + weft mesh) ÷ 228.6
BOPP film GSM = micron × 0.92 (e.g. 18 µm ≈ 16.6 GSM, 20 µm ≈ 18.4 GSM, 25 µm ≈ 23.0 GSM)
Laminated GSM = fabric GSM + coating GSM + BOPP film GSM
Bag weight = tube width × cut length ÷ 10,000 × laminated GSM + liner + thread (+ patches for block bottom)

Typical BOPP bag specifications

  • Rice, flour, sugar 5–25 kg: 800–900 denier, 10 × 10 mesh, 18–20 GSM coating, 18–20 µm BOPP, open mouth stitched or pinch bottom.
  • Pet food, seeds, fertiliser: 900–1000 denier, 12 × 12 mesh, 20–25 µm BOPP, often block bottom with gusset.
  • Metallised BOPP: same weight as clear BOPP of the same micron; the metallising layer is negligible in grams.

BOPP bag costing

BOPP film and extrusion coating resin are priced per kilogram and move with the polymer market, so a BOPP bag quote is only as good as the rate date behind it. FAQ

How do I convert BOPP micron to GSM?

Multiply micron by the film factor, 0.92 for standard BOPP (some films run 0.90). 18 micron ≈ 16.6 GSM, 20 micron ≈ 18.4 GSM, 25 micron ≈ 23.0 GSM. Nexora keeps this factor in the Constants Master so a plant can set its own.

What coating GSM is used for BOPP lamination?

Extrusion coating for BOPP lamination is typically 15–22 GSM of PP or LDPE-based resin. Enter your plant's figure in the coating field.

Does printing change the weight?

Reverse printing adds ink over the laminated area. The calculator uses the app default of 1.5 GSM.
