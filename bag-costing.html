Bag costing guide

Bag Costing for PP Woven Sacks & Packaging

Bag costing is working out what one bag costs you to make — raw material plus process-wise conversion plus waste — before the quotation goes out. This page sets out the woven sack cost sheet line by line, the formula behind each line, a worked example, and what bag costing software has to do that a spreadsheet cannot.

Open the bag costing calculator

What is bag costing?

Bag costing is the calculation that turns a bag specification into a cost per bag. A specification — size, mesh, denier, GSM, lamination, print, bottom type — decides how many grams of each material the bag consumes. Rates and process costs turn those grams into rupees. Everything else in a quotation, from margin to freight, sits on top of that one number.

It is worth being precise about the two figures people mix up. Cost per kilogram is what the market quotes and what a supplier price list carries. Cost per bag is what decides whether the order makes money, because the customer buys bags. The bridge between them is the weight of the bag, which is why bag costing always starts with weight and never with price.

specification → bag weight (g) → kg per bag (with waste) → × rates → cost per bag

Packaging costing is the same discipline applied across the pack: sacks, liners, BOPP laminates, stitched or pasted bottoms, and the printing on them. The lines change with the construction; the method does not.

2. Conversion cost, process by process

Conversion is what it costs to run the material through your plant: power, manpower, machine hours, consumables and the overhead attached to each. It is carried per kilogram at each process, not as one factory-wide figure, because a plain stitched sack and a printed BOPP bag do not use the same machines.

3. What sits on top

  • Process waste — start-up, edge trim, print make-ready, rejected bags. Scrap is bought and paid for, so waste belongs inside the cost, not in a contingency at the end.
  • Factory overhead — rent, supervision, maintenance, quality; usually apportioned per kilogram of saleable output.
  • Freight and packing to the customer, where the quotation is delivered rather than ex-works.
  • Finance and credit — 60 or 90 day terms on a resin-heavy product are a real cost of the order.
  • Margin, applied last and applied knowingly.

Bag costing formula

This is the woven sack costing formula every bag costing software has to get right, whatever it looks like on the screen.

fabric GSM     = denier × (mesh + mesh) ÷ 228.6
laminated GSM = fabric GSM + coating GSM + (micron × 0.92) + ink GSM
bag weight (g) = cut width × cut length ÷ 10,000 × laminated GSM + patches + valve + thread
kg per bag    = bag weight ÷ 1000 × (1 + waste %)
material cost  = Σ (each material kg × its effective rate)
conversion    = Σ (kg at each process × that process rate per kg)
cost per bag  = material + conversion + overhead + freight
selling price  = cost per bag ÷ (1 − margin %)   — margin on price, not on cost

What that table tells you before any rate is applied

  • The woven fabric dwarfs every other line. A GSM argument is a cost argument; an argument about the ink rate is not.
  • Coating and film weigh more than their GSM suggests, because both run 10 mm wider than the body — a small percentage on the two most expensive lines in the bag.
  • Waste is raw material bought and never sold. It is small per bag and large per order, and it differs by process — which is why one flat percentage across the whole bag under-costs a printed laminate.
  • Five grams of specification is about half a tonne of extra raw material on an order of 1,00,000 bags — before anyone argues about a rate.

Where spreadsheet bag costing goes wrong

Almost every plant starts with an Excel bag costing sheet, and it works until it quietly does not. The failures are consistent:

Rates are typed in and never datedNobody can say what rate an approved quotation was costed at, so a resin move silently rewrites old numbers

What bag costing software has to do

A calculator answers one question. A bag costing system has to hold the answer together across a year of price moves and a hundred enquiries. In practice that means:

  • Weight from specification, not from memory — component-wise, with the formula visible against every intermediate value.
  • A dated price master — every rate a version with an effective-from date, so an approved costing keeps the rate it was costed at.
  • Process-wise conversion cost and process-wise waste, so each construction is charged only for the machines it touches.
  • Cost per bag and cost per kilogram side by side, always both, never one.
  • A costed BOM that becomes the production BOM, so nothing is re-keyed between quotation and shop-floor.
  • An order-level forecast — the kilograms of each material an order will consume, before it is accepted.
  • Confidentiality — rates are the most sensitive number in the plant and should be visible by role.

That is what Nexora bag costing software does, across 29 bag constructions, offline on a shop-floor PC, with a 3D view of the structure and an ink assumption model for printed work. Frequently asked questions about bag costing

The cost sheet

What is a bag costing cost sheet?

A cost sheet is the one page that shows how a bag's price was built: every raw material with its weight and rate, every process with the kilograms passing through it and its rate per kilogram, then waste, overhead, freight and margin. Its purpose is not the total at the bottom — it is that any line can be questioned. A quotation without a cost sheet behind it is a guess with a decimal point.

How do I cost a woven sack step by step?

Five steps. One, fix the specification — size, mesh, denier or GSM, coating, film micron, print, bottom type. Two, turn the specification into the weight of each component. Three, add process waste to get the kilograms one bag consumes. Four, multiply each material by its dated rate and add the conversion cost of each process the bag actually visits. Five, add overhead and freight, then apply margin on selling price. The bag costing calculator does steps one to three for you.

What is the difference between bag costing and packaging costing?

Bag costing is one article. Packaging costing is the whole pack — the sack, its liner, the label, the print, and sometimes the bale and the pallet the bags ship on. The method is identical; packaging costing just has more articles and usually a customer-specific combination of them, which is why it belongs in a costed Bill of Materials rather than in a single sheet.

How do I add factory overhead to a bag cost?

Apportion it per kilogram of saleable output, not per bag and not as a percentage of material. Total the overheads that are not already inside a process rate — rent, supervision, quality, general maintenance — and divide by the kilograms you actually sell in a normal month. A percentage of material cost moves the overhead every time resin moves, which is the wrong behaviour.

Should margin be calculated on cost or on selling price?

On selling price. Cost ÷ (1 − margin %) gives the price that leaves you the margin you intended. Adding a percentage to cost gives a smaller margin than the number suggests — add 20% to cost and you have earned 16.7% of the price, not 20%. On a high-volume, thin-margin product that difference is most of the profit.

How do I include freight in a bag quotation?

Work it per bag, not per consignment. Bags per bale, bales per truck and the truck rate to that destination give a rupee-per-bag freight that can sit as a line in the cost sheet. Quoting ex-works and absorbing freight later is how a profitable order becomes a break-even one on a long haul.

Rates, waste and recosting

What is an effective raw material rate?

The rate a material actually costs you once everything around the invoice is accounted for: the basic rate, less any discount, plus inward freight and handling, and with recycled or reprocessed content blended in at its own rate. Costing on the printed invoice rate alone is optimistic by exactly the amount you spend getting the material into the plant.

How do I recost quotations when the resin price changes?

Do not recost the approved ones. An approved quotation must keep the rate it was costed at, or you lose the ability to explain a price to a customer or to yourself. Instead give every rate an effective-from date, so new calculations pick up the new rate automatically while old ones stay intact, and run a comparison to see which live quotations are now below cost.

How does process waste differ from one process to another?

Considerably, which is why one flat figure misleads. Extrusion and weaving waste comes from start-up and edge trim and is usually low. Lamination adds its own start-up and joint loss. Printing is the heaviest, because make-ready, colour matching and cylinder changes scrap finished laminate — the most expensive material in the bag. Cost each process with its own waste and a printed BOPP bag stops looking cheaper than it is.

How do I cost the ink on a printed bag?

From coverage, not from a flat allowance. Each colour covers a percentage of the printed area, and ink laid down per square metre depends on that coverage, the substrate and how much solid white is behind it. Multiply area by coverage by the ink laid down, per colour, then add solvent. Nexora has an ink assumption model that reads coverage from the artwork itself and reports how confident it is — see the ink assumption module.

How do I cost a bag with a liner?

As two articles in one Bill of Materials. The liner has its own lay-flat width, cut length, micron or gauge, its own material rate and its own conversion, and it is inserted as a separate operation with its own labour. Use the LDPE liner weight calculator for the liner weight, then add it as a line in the bag's cost sheet.

Does scrap or reprocessed material reduce the cost of a bag?

It reduces the rate, not the weight, and only up to the percentage your recipe and your customer allow. Cost it honestly: the blended rate is the virgin rate and the reprocessed rate weighted by the recipe, and the recipe has to be the one the plant actually runs, not the one the costing sheet assumes.

Tools and software

Can I do bag costing in Excel?

You can, and most plants start there. It holds up while one person owns the sheet and rates are stable. It stops holding up when rates need dates, when each enquiry spawns a copy, when waste has to differ by process, and when the costing has to become a purchase plan. At that point the spreadsheet is no longer a calculation, it is six versions of one.

Is there free bag costing software?

The calculators on this site are free and need no sign-up — the bag costing calculator gives you the weight and shows how the cost is built from example rates. What free tools do not give you is the part that makes costing reliable over a year: your own dated rates, process-wise conversion, component-wise weights, and a costed BOM that carries into production. That is what the desktop app is for.

How accurate is a bag costing calculator compared with a plant costing?

The weight is close, because geometry is geometry. The money is indicative, because it runs on example rates and common defaults for fold, patch, valve, waste and conversion. A calculator is the right tool to check a quotation or catch a mistake. A quotation you will be held to should run on your own constants, your own dated rates and your own process costs.

Why are the costing figures on this site blurred?

The bag weight is yours to read freely. The money and the component-wise break-up are shown on request, because those figures depend on rates and process costs that have to be set up properly to mean anything. Request a demo and we will run your own specification with your own rates.

Does bag costing connect to purchase and production?

It should. The costing already knows the kilograms of every material one bag consumes, so multiplying by the order quantity gives the purchase requirement without anybody re-keying it. In Nexora the costed Bill of Materials becomes the production BOM, and the order forecast gives the raw material an order will need before it is accepted.

Who in a woven sack plant should own bag costing?

Whoever is accountable for margin, with the technical team owning the specification. In practice costing sits between sales and production and belongs to neither, which is why it drifts. Putting the constants, the rates and the constructions in one system with roles and approvals is what stops each department carrying its own version of the truth.
